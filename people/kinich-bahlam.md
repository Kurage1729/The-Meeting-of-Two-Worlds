K’inich Bahlam was a Maya king who unified the divided Maya region in 1838 and established the political foundations of the restored Kingdom of the Maya.

## Unification

Following the expulsion of the Mexica in 1831, the Maya region remained divided between the Mérida Maya Kingdom in northern Yucatán and the Petén Maya Kingdom in Petén and the Guatemalan highlands. K’inich Bahlam, ruler of the Petén Maya Kingdom, sought to reunify the Maya territories.

With the support of the powerful K’anul family of Tiho (Mérida), he entered the city in 1838 and defeated the Mérida government. The Pech family, which had supported the previous regime, lost much of its political influence. K’inich Bahlam subsequently established himself as ruler of a unified Maya kingdom.

## Restoration policies

K’inich Bahlam presented his rule as a restoration of ancient Maya political and cultural traditions. He revived the Maya script, which had been abolished under the previous Mérida government, and restored human sacrifice in northern Yucatán according to traditions maintained in Petén.

His restoration policies gained considerable support in rural Maya communities, but were deeply unpopular among the urban elite of Tiho. K’inich Bahlam therefore spent much of his reign at Mayapán rather than in Tiho.

In 1844, K’inich Bahlam was assassinated. He was succeeded by Jasaw Chan Yopaat.
