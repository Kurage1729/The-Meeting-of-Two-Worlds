Jasaw Chan Yopaat was a Maya king who consolidated the restored kingdom after the assassination of K’inich Bahlam. His reign marked the transition from cultural restoration to centralized royal government.

## The Mérida Accord

In 1847, Jasaw Chan Yopaat concluded the Mérida Accord with the Pech family, ending the political conflict between the monarchy and the influential aristocratic house. Among other provisions, the agreement abolished human sacrifice, reversing one of the most controversial restoration policies of K’inich Bahlam.

The agreement was followed almost immediately by a rebellion against the Maya king in Tiho. Jasaw Chan Yopaat suppressed the uprising and strengthened the authority of the central government.

## Suppression of republicanism

In 1855, republican rebels influenced by the Mexica Republican Revolution rose against the Maya monarchy. Jasaw Chan Yopaat suppressed the uprising. The defeat of the republican movement greatly strengthened the political position of the monarchy.

During the remainder of his reign, Jasaw Chan Yopaat progressively restricted the privileges of the aristocracy and concentrated political authority in the hands of the king. His reign therefore laid the foundations for the autocratic monarchy that dominated the Maya kingdom in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Jasaw Chan Yopaat was succeeded by Muwaan Jol in 1871.

*Jasaw Chan Yopaat's political career is commonly compared to that of Rafael Carrera in real-world Central American history.*
