# First World War in the Americas

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > First World War in the Americas

The First World War spread directly into North America after the Third Mexica Empire entered the conflict on the side of the Central Powers in 1916. Mexica declared war on the United States under the banner of liberating the Indigenous peoples of the Americas.

## Mexica strategy

Porforio Savi accepted the proposal associated with German foreign minister Arthur Zimmermann and presented the war as a continental Indigenous struggle. Mexica forces quickly occupied Pueblo territory and advanced into Texas and the American Southwest.

Several southwestern kingdoms rebelled after receiving promises of postwar independence. The Pueblo government joined the Central Powers after Mexica guaranteed its restoration.

## American response

Mexica planners expected Indigenous communities throughout the United States to revolt. Instead, most Indigenous peoples in the eastern United States supported the federal government. This prevented a wider collapse and allowed the United States to stabilize the front, counterattack, and eventually occupy Tenochtitlan.

The United States also entered the European theater.

## Maya entry

K’an Chitam had once supported Savi's Pan-Americanism but rejected its increasingly Mexica-centered form. The Kingdom of the Maya declared war on Mexica in 1917 and joined the Allied side.

## Consequences

Mexica was defeated in 1918. The Third Empire was abolished, the Second Republic was created, and Savi was confined to an estate outside Tenochtitlan. Mexica retained its territory but faced long-term reparations and military restrictions.

Within the United States, the loyalty of most Indigenous citizens increased their political influence and accelerated movements against voting restrictions. In the Maya kingdom, military victory strengthened the armed forces, while disappointment with the postwar settlement contributed to the [6 B’en Revolution](6-ben-revolution.md).
