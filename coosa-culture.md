# Coosa Culture

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Coosa culture

The Coosa Kingdom was a Creek-speaking state of the Mississippi cultural region. In TMTW, it took the Southeastern traditions known from real history (platform mounds, divided towns, the Green Corn Ceremony, and the ball game) and developed them on a much larger scale. This article describes the distinctive monumental, political, and ceremonial culture that resulted.

## Mounds

In real history, the Mississippian peoples of the Southeast built earthen platform mounds as bases for temples and elite residences. In TMTW, the mounds of the Coosa royal capitals grew far larger than their historical counterparts. The greatest of them were reinforced with stone retaining walls and lime plaster, so that their lower stages resemble fortress walls more than earthworks.

The highest was the **Red Mound**, which reached 70 meters including the timber palace on its summit. It was destroyed during the Coosa War, and the White Mound is now the tallest surviving mound of the kingdom.

Parts of the walls carry inscriptions in the [Mississippi scripts](writing-systems.md#mississippi-scripts). These record the names of kings, officials, and carpenters, together with lists of military campaigns. The mounds therefore served not only as seats of government and religion but also as public archives in stone.

## The White and Red Capitals

In real history, Muskogean towns were divided into **white towns**, associated with peace, diplomacy, and mediation under a civil chief, and **red towns**, associated with war and military affairs under a war chief.

In TMTW, the same division existed on a far larger scale. The kingdom had two capitals, the **White Capital** and the **Red Capital**, and government was shared between the **White King** and the **Red King**. The White King's side handled law, diplomacy, and arbitration, while the Red King's side handled war and the army.

## The sacred fire

A sacred fire burned at the center of the plaza of the White Capital. During the Green Corn Ceremony in summer, the kingdom held the **New Fire Ceremony**. The old fires were put out, and a new fire was kindled from the sacred fire. It was first distributed to the regions of the kingdom and from there to individual households, so that every hearth in the kingdom was renewed from the same source.

## Copper roofs

Copper was a prestige good in real Mississippian societies, used mainly for ornaments and ceremonial plates. As the Coosa state grew in wealth and power, copper came to be used for roofing important buildings, especially on the mounds.

Decorated roofs were common. Ridges and gables were often topped with figures of birds, deer, and snakes. These are frequently interpreted as references to the upper world, this world, and the underworld of Southeastern cosmology.

## Stickball

Stickball, called the "little brother of war" in real history, was played throughout the Southeast. In Coosa, it was promoted as a means of binding together the countries within the royal domain. The ball ground at the center of the Red Capital was the most prestigious in the kingdom.
