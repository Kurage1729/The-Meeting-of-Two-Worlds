## Overview

The Kingdom of the Maya includes Guatemala, Belize, Yucatán, Quintana Roo, Campeche, and eastern Chiapas. It is a predominantly Maya country in which Maya writing remains in everyday use. Pride in the antiquity of Maya civilization is an important part of national identity, and the Mexica are sometimes described as historical newcomers.

## From regional states to a united Maya realm

Eurasian diseases reached the Maya region in the mid-twelfth century and contributed to the rapid decline of Chichen Itza. Horses arrived during the fourteenth century and wheat during the fifteenth. Both were especially useful in the Guatemalan highlands, strengthening states such as K’iche’.

The Mexica conquered the divided northern lowlands around 1500 but failed to conquer the highlands after resistance led by the K’iche’ hero Tecún Umán. A lowland rebellion supported by K’iche’ and Spain expelled Mexica rule in the 1540s.

Around 1560, the K’iche’ king formed a united Maya imperial confederation and adopted the title *Kaloomte’*. The state used Classical Maya as its written language and gradually adopted more lowland cultural features. Mayapán was rebuilt as the capital around 1620.

## Spanish campaigns and the Petén kingdom

Spain ended its alliance with the Maya and conquered the Guatemalan highlands during the 1710s and 1720s. The northern lowlands survived longer through an alliance with Britain, but British support declined after the defeat at Cartagena in 1741. Spain conquered the northern lowlands later in the decade.

The court withdrew to Nojpetén, after which the surviving state was known as the Petén Maya Kingdom. Britain continued to support it as a defense for British Belize, discouraging further large Spanish expeditions.

## Independence and restoration

In 1822, colonial officials, landowners, and military leaders in Guatemala City and Tiho accepted annexation by the Mexica Empire. Maya nationalists rebelled. In 1831, Maya forces expelled the Mexica army and invited the Petén Maya king K’inich Bahlam to rule a restored kingdom from Tiho.

The new kingdom demanded British withdrawal from Belize, beginning a territorial dispute that lasted into the twentieth century.

## Monarchy and reform

The aristocracy initially dominated the restored kingdom. A ruler provisionally identified as King A destroyed the powerful House Alpha in 1847, suppressed a republican uprising in 1855, and greatly reduced aristocratic privileges in 1865. His successor, provisionally identified as King B, promoted modernization and coffee exports after 1871.

K’an Chitam came to the throne in 1888. He initially supported Savi's Pan-Americanism and promoted Classical Maya revival and linguistic purification. As Savi's movement became increasingly Mexica-centered, K’an Chitam moved toward the United States. The Maya entered the First World War against Mexica in 1917.

The [6 B’en Revolution](../history/6-ben-revolution.md) of 1922 restored parliament, restricted royal power, ended Classical Maya as the official literary language, and abolished aristocratic cranial modification. A military ruler provisionally identified as C established an authoritarian regime in 1931. Mass protests removed him in 1944 and reduced the monarchy to a symbolic institution.

## Language and writing

Standard Maya is based on the Yucatec language of Tiho. Classical Maya roots were used extensively to create technical vocabulary. A late nineteenth-century purification movement replaced most Spanish-derived learned vocabulary. The modern national script combines a standardized missionary orthography with elements of the older court tradition.
