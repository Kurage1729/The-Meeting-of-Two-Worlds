# Mesoamerican Customs

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Mesoamerican customs

This article describes practices that survived far longer in TMTW because Mesoamerican states and elite institutions remained intact into the modern period.

## Artificial cranial modification

Artificial cranial modification reshapes an infant's skull with boards or bands while the bone is still soft. In real history, the practice declined after the sixteenth-century conquest. In TMTW it survived into modern times.

Spanish colonial authorities attempted to prohibit it during the eighteenth century but abandoned enforcement in the face of strong local opposition. In Mexica it declined rapidly after the Republican Revolution of 1855. In the Maya kingdom it remained a mark of royal and aristocratic status into the twentieth century.

Maya intellectuals and European observers strongly criticized the practice. The 6 B’en Revolution of 1922 forced K’an Chitam to abolish it among the royal family and nobility. Some remote communities continue the practice in the present day.

## Human sacrifice

Human sacrifice also survived longer than in real history, especially in remote regions. Spanish colonial law officially prohibited it, but authorities sometimes ignored ceremonies performed by powerful nobles.

Both Mexica and the Maya prohibited the practice after independence. During the 1830s, however, a priest related to the Mexica imperial family was discovered to have sacrificed a child to Tlaloc. The scandal became one of the sources of opposition to the Second Empire.

By the twentieth century, human sacrifice was believed to have disappeared. In 1950, a United Nations investigation discovered that it continued in parts of the Petén region. The Maya government launched investigations, arrested priests, and conducted public education campaigns. The practice is no longer believed to occur.

## Obsidian

Iron gradually replaced obsidian in ordinary warfare, but obsidian retained ritual and political symbolism. The assassin in the [4 Kimi Incident](../history/4-kimi-incident.md) deliberately used an obsidian knife, giving the attack a connection to older Mesoamerican traditions.
