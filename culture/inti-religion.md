# Inti Religion

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Inti religion

The Inti religion is an Andean faith that developed from historical Inkarri beliefs. It recognizes Inti as the highest deity and teaches that the Sapa Inca will one day return to save the people.

## Formation

The religion emerged in an organized form around 1700, after the destruction of the last New Inca state. Its scriptures were written in Quechua using the Latin alphabet, while quipu played an important role in ritual.

The movement was heavily influenced by Christianity. Its teaching about a returning Sapa Inca and its accommodation with localized Catholic practice made it difficult for colonial authorities to define and suppress. In many communities, the boundary between Inti practice and local Catholicism remained unclear.

## Political role

Túpac Amaru III's uprising of 1780–1783 attempted a radical restoration of Inca institutions, including quipu administration. Its failure later encouraged more moderate Inti political leaders.

José Mariano Chuquisengo established a moderate Inti republic in Peru after independence. He refused to claim the title Sapa Inca and balanced religious legitimacy against modern government. In 1826, he established the Sacred Quipu script as a compromise between doctrine and administrative practicality.

The religion became a major source of common identity for Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador during the formation of the [Andean Federation](../countries/andean-federation.md).

## Reform and secularization

The Inti Church and the federal government were initially strongly Quechua-centered. Aymara and other revolts during the 1850s challenged this structure. The federation later reorganized into ethnic and regional states.

During the 1890s, the state restricted the political authority of the Inti Church and introduced freedom of religion and secular education.
