# Human Sacrifice

This article concerns the practice of human sacrifice in Mesoamerica.

In real history, human sacrifice declined rapidly after the 16th century under Spanish rule. In this timeline, however, it persisted in remote regions until the 20th century.

During the Spanish colonial period, colonial authorities officially prohibited the practice. Nevertheless, they occasionally tolerated human sacrifices conducted by influential nobles.

After achieving independence, both Mexica and Maya outlawed human sacrifice.

In the 1830s, it was revealed that a priest related to Emperor Ihuitemotzin of Mexica had sacrificed a child to Tlaloc. The scandal became one of several criticisms directed against the monarchy.

By the 20th century, the practice was generally believed to have disappeared. However, in the 1950s, a United Nations investigative mission discovered evidence of human sacrifice in the Petén region of Maya, causing an international controversy.

The royal government subsequently launched a campaign to eradicate the practice through field investigations, the arrest of priests involved, and public education efforts. Human sacrifice is no longer practiced today.
