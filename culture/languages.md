# Languages of the Americas

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Languages

The survival of large Indigenous populations and states allowed many American languages to become modern national and administrative languages. They possess standardized forms, technical vocabulary, mass media, and established systems of education.

## Nahuatl

Nahuatl is an official language of [Mexica](../countries/mexica.md). Its dialects have approximately 90 million first-language speakers and 70 million second-language speakers. It is also widely spoken by Mexica migrant communities, especially in the southwestern United States.

The standard language is based on the Tenochtitlan dialect and is used in newspapers and broadcasting. The Latin alphabet is the normal writing system, while Mexica writing sometimes appears in ceremonies and public monuments.

Its grammar does not differ radically from real-world Nahuatl, but it has a large modern administrative and technical vocabulary. Spanish loans remain common in learned registers.

## Standard Maya

Standard Maya is the official language of the [Kingdom of the Maya](../countries/maya.md). It has approximately 8 million first-language speakers, but almost the entire population of roughly 40 million can use it as a first or second language.

The standard is based on Yucatec Maya, especially the dialect of Tiho. It is normally written in the modern national Maya script, which is used in books and newspapers.

Its grammar remains broadly comparable to real-world Maya languages. Technical and administrative terms were created largely from Classical Maya roots, especially Ch’orti’-related material. This produced many pairs of everyday and learned words. A late nineteenth-century language purification movement replaced most Spanish loanwords in learned vocabulary.

## Vinlandic

Vinlandic is the official language of the [Kingdom of Vinland](../countries/vinland.md). It has approximately 30 million first-language speakers and 7 million second-language speakers.

The language descends from Old Norse as spoken by settlers who arrived around the year 1000. A millennium of contact with Algonquian and other North American languages produced features uncommon in European languages, including polysynthetic verbal structures and possessive prefixes.

Vinlandic is normally written in North American runes, although the Latin alphabet is also used. Much modern vocabulary has been borrowed from French and English.

## English

English is the official language of the [United States](../countries/united-states.md). It contains a large number of regional varieties shaped by Creek, Illinois, and many other Indigenous languages. The strongest varieties can be difficult for speakers of more international forms of English to understand.

## Other major languages

Spanish, Quechua, Mapuche, Guaraní, Nheengatu, and many North American Indigenous languages hold national, state, or regional status. Their detailed profiles remain to be expanded.
