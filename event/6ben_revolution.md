# 6 B'en Revolution

The **6 B'en Revolution** was a political revolution that took place in the Maya Kingdom on October 28, 1922.

Following the end of the First World War, K’an Chitam's absolute monarchy was increasingly viewed as outdated. Critics also pointed to the government's failure to recover unredeemed Maya territories—particularly central Tabasco—despite Maya's status as one of the victorious powers.

On October 28, 1922, thousands of citizens gathered in Tiho demanding the promulgation of a new constitution and broader political reforms. The revolution takes its name from the corresponding Tzolk'in date, **6 B'en**.

K’an Chitam requested military assistance from the United States. However, with the defeat of Mexica in the First World War, the United States no longer regarded Maya as an indispensable ally and declined to intervene.

Ultimately, the king was forced to concede to many of the revolutionaries' demands and abdicated in favor of his son.

The revolution resulted in:

* The restoration of the long-dormant Maya parliament.
* The promulgation of a new constitution.
* The abolition of Classical Maya as the official literary language.
* The abolition of artificial cranial deformation among the royal family and nobility.
