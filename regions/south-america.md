# South America

[Main page](../index.md) > [Regions](index.md) > South America

South America was more distant from the first Norse settlements, so horses, wheat, and ironworking arrived later and had a less uniform impact. Outside the Andes, the rise of large centralized states before European contact remained limited.

Eurasian diseases nevertheless arrived before large-scale European colonization. Indigenous populations therefore avoided the sudden demographic collapse seen in real history. Colonial governments faced numerous surviving communities and political traditions, while independent states inherited complex ethnic boundaries, land disputes, and regional power structures.

## The Andes

Spain invaded the Inca realm in 1532. Cusco fell in 1570, but successor states descended from Vilcabamba continued resistance until 1630. During the colonial period, Inkarri beliefs developed into the Inti religion. This movement later provided an important source of identity for the independent Andean states and their union in the [Andean Federation](../countries/andean-federation.md).

## Eastern and southern South America

Portuguese America fragmented into Brazil, Amazonia, Riograndense, and a Guaraní state that later united with Spanish Paraguay. Large Indigenous autonomous territories also exist in Chile and Argentina. European migration was redirected away from the United States, contributing to a particularly large European-descended population in Argentina.
