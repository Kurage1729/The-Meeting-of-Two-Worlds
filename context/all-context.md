# The Meeting of Two Worlds

*The Meeting of Two Worlds* (TMTW) is an alternate-history setting in which Norse settlers established a permanent colony in North America in the early eleventh century. Their arrival introduced Eurasian diseases, horses, Old World crops, and ironworking centuries before Columbus.

Because epidemics and technological exchange began much earlier, the Indigenous societies of the Americas followed a very different historical path. Powerful states survived into the colonial period, European conquests took far longer, and many Indigenous languages, writing systems, religions, and political institutions remain central to the modern Americas.

# World Overview

[Main page](index.md) > World overview

## Point of divergence

In the early eleventh century, Norse settlers reached Newfoundland. Unlike in real history, they arrived with enough livestock, seed grain, ironworkers, and other specialists to establish a permanent settlement. Although contact with Europe later weakened, their communities survived and expanded into Labrador, the St. Lawrence basin, and the Great Lakes.

The settlers also brought Eurasian pathogens. Epidemics therefore struck the Americas centuries before Columbus, but repeated exposure meant that much of the population had acquired some collective resistance by the beginning of large-scale European colonization.

## Continental transformation

The Norse settlements became the starting point for the spread of horses, wheat, and ironworking. These did not move south at the same speed. Geography, access to raw materials, political stability, and local demand all shaped their adoption.

The earliest effects were felt in North America. Norse-derived states appeared along the Mississippi, while states in the Great Lakes, the Plains, the Pueblo region, and the Northwest Coast developed in response to new military and economic conditions. Mesoamerican states later incorporated horses, wheat, and iron into already sophisticated political systems. South America was more distant, so the effects outside the Andes remained more uneven.

## European conquest

The Indigenous states encountered by European colonizers were more populous, technologically stronger, and politically more resilient than their real-world counterparts. Spain failed to conquer the Mexica Empire in the sixteenth century and did not take Tenochtitlan permanently until 1697. Maya resistance continued even later in parts of the lowlands.

In the Andes, the Inca state collapsed only after decades of war. Successor regimes continued resistance until 1630. Because Indigenous populations did not undergo the same catastrophic post-contact decline as in real history, colonial administrations had to rule through surviving communities, nobles, and local institutions.

## The modern Americas

The result is a modern hemisphere in which Indigenous peoples form majorities in many countries. Pre-Columbian political traditions coexist with republics, constitutional monarchies, and autonomous ethnic territories. Nahuatl, Maya, Vinlandic, Quechua, Guaraní, Nheengatu, and numerous North American languages are used in government, education, and mass media. Maya writing, North American runes, Mississippi-derived scripts, and the Sacred Quipu script remain living or ceremonial writing systems.

# Mesoamerica

[Main page](../index.md) > [Regions](index.md) > Mesoamerica

Mesoamerica already contained large, urbanized states before the arrival of Eurasian technologies. In TMTW, the adoption of horses, wheat, and iron allowed these states to resist European conquest for far longer than in real history.

## Before European arrival

Iron objects first appeared during a Vinlandic raid on the Huastec region around 1200. Trade in Mississippi iron developed during the early thirteenth century, and Tarascan smiths began producing iron locally in the early fourteenth century. By the arrival of the Spanish, iron was common, although obsidian retained military and ritual importance.

The Mexica used horses and easily transported wheat supplies to expand beyond their real-world limits. Under Ahuitzotl they conquered Tarascan territory and much of the northern Maya lowlands. Under Moctezuma II they brought much of Aridoamerica into a tributary system.

## Spanish conquest

The first Spanish expedition failed to take Tenochtitlan. Reforms under Cuitláhuac reduced tribute and granted greater autonomy to subject peoples, preventing Spain from recreating the alliance system that had destroyed the historical Aztec Empire.

War continued intermittently for almost two centuries. Tenochtitlan fell temporarily in 1600, was recovered with Dutch support in the 1620s, and fell permanently in 1697. Spanish campaigns against the Maya continued into the eighteenth century, while the Petén Maya kingdom survived with British support.

## Modern Mesoamerica

Colonial authorities ruled through surviving nobles and local communities. After independence, both Mexica and Maya state builders presented themselves as heirs to pre-Columbian civilization. Nahuatl and Standard Maya became national languages, while the Mexica and Maya writing traditions continued in public, religious, or everyday use.

# North America

[Main page](../index.md) > [Regions](index.md) > North America

North America outside Mesoamerica was the first part of the continent to experience the effects of permanent Norse settlement. The Great Lakes and Mississippi basin became corridors for the spread of pathogens, horses, wheat, and ironworking.

## Early development

Norse settlers moved through Labrador and the St. Lawrence basin before reaching the Great Lakes. The Kingdom of Vinland was founded at Mackinac around 1120. From the mid-twelfth century, Vinlandic expeditions moved down the Mississippi and established several states along the main river. Their ruling groups were numerically small and were eventually absorbed by local populations, but they helped transmit Old World crops, metalworking, and new trade routes.

The resulting political landscape differed sharply from that of real history. Centralized states survived or arose in the Mississippi basin, the Pueblo region, the Plains, and the Northwest Coast. They negotiated, fought, and formed alliances with France, Britain, Spain, Russia, Vinland, and the United States.

## European and American expansion

France gained influence in the Great Lakes and Mississippi regions, while Britain later absorbed many French possessions. The United States expanded westward much more slowly because it faced established Indigenous kingdoms rather than sparsely defended territory. Many of these states were eventually incorporated into the United States, often after prolonged wars or negotiated arrangements.

Their legacies survive in the modern federal system. The United States contains a Confederated State, Indigenous Crowned States, and numerous national counties. Vinland also contains autonomous governments, including the northern Haudenosaunee government and the Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom.

# South America

[Main page](../index.md) > [Regions](index.md) > South America

South America was more distant from the first Norse settlements, so horses, wheat, and ironworking arrived later and had a less uniform impact. Outside the Andes, the rise of large centralized states before European contact remained limited.

Eurasian diseases nevertheless arrived before large-scale European colonization. Indigenous populations therefore avoided the sudden demographic collapse seen in real history. Colonial governments faced numerous surviving communities and political traditions, while independent states inherited complex ethnic boundaries, land disputes, and regional power structures.

## The Andes

Spain invaded the Inca realm in 1532. Cusco fell in 1570, but successor states descended from Vilcabamba continued resistance until 1630. During the colonial period, Inkarri beliefs developed into the Inti religion. This movement later provided an important source of identity for the independent Andean states and their union in the [Andean Federation](../countries/andean-federation.md).

## Eastern and southern South America

Portuguese America fragmented into Brazil, Amazonia, Riograndense, and a Guaraní state that later united with Spanish Paraguay. Large Indigenous autonomous territories also exist in Chile and Argentina. European migration was redirected away from the United States, contributing to a particularly large European-descended population in Argentina.

# Andean Federation

[Main page](../index.md) > [Countries](index.md) > Andean Federation

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Government | Federal republic |
| Capital | Lima |
| Population | Approximately 66 million |
| Indigenous population | Approximately 80% |
| Languages | Spanish, Quechua, and others |
| Territory | Peru, Bolivia, Ecuador, Tarapacá, Arica, and Acre |

## Overview

The Andean Federation unites most of the former Inca world. Its common identity developed partly through the [Inti religion](../culture/inti-religion.md), which grew out of Inkarri beliefs and taught that the Sapa Inca would one day return to save the people.

## Spanish conquest and resistance

Spain invaded the Inca Empire in 1532. Cusco fell in 1570, ending the unified Inca state, but successor regimes connected to Vilcabamba continued resistance. The last New Inca state was destroyed in 1630, when Túpac Amaru II was killed.

Around 1700, the Inti religion developed a Quechua sacred literature in the Latin alphabet and a ritual tradition using quipu. In 1780, José Gabriel Condorcanqui proclaimed himself Túpac Amaru III and occupied Cusco. His attempt to restore quipu administration and other radical institutions disrupted administration and alliances, allowing Spain to defeat him in 1783.

## Independence

José Mariano Chuquisengo declared independence in the Peruvian highlands in 1821. After failing to advance on Lima, he requested assistance from Bolívar in 1823. Peruvian independence was secured in 1824, creating a moderate Inti republic. Bolivia became independent in 1825 and Ecuador in 1830.

The Sacred Quipu script was established in 1826 as a compromise between religious doctrine and the requirements of a modern state. Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador formed the Alliance of Andean Republics in 1831 and united as the Andean Federation in 1838.

## Federal consolidation

Chile and Andean opponents of federation invaded between 1839 and 1841. Chile briefly occupied Lima but could not hold it, allowing the federation to survive.

Aymara and other uprisings during the 1850s exposed the Quechua-centered character of both the Inti Church and the state. In the 1870s, the three original republics were abolished and replaced by dozens of ethnic and regional states.

## Territorial conflicts and secularization

During the War of the Pacific of 1879–1884, the federation lost Antofagasta but retained Tarapacá and Arica. In the 1890s, it restricted the political authority of the Inti Church and introduced religious freedom and secular education.

During the Acre conflict of 1899–1902, the federation defeated a separatist movement led by settlers from Amazonia and retained Acre as a federal state.

# Kingdom of the Maya

[Main page](../index.md) > [Countries](index.md) > Kingdom of the Maya

## Overview

The Kingdom of the Maya includes Guatemala, Belize, Yucatán, Quintana Roo, Campeche, and eastern Chiapas. It is a predominantly Maya country in which Maya writing remains in everyday use. Pride in the antiquity of Maya civilization is an important part of national identity, and the Mexica are sometimes described as historical newcomers.

## From regional states to a united Maya realm

Eurasian diseases reached the Maya region in the mid-twelfth century and contributed to the rapid decline of Chichen Itza. Horses arrived during the fourteenth century and wheat during the fifteenth. Both were especially useful in the Guatemalan highlands, strengthening states such as K’iche’.

The Mexica conquered the divided northern lowlands around 1500 but failed to conquer the highlands after resistance led by the K’iche’ hero Tecún Umán. A lowland rebellion supported by K’iche’ and Spain expelled Mexica rule in the 1540s.

Around 1560, the K’iche’ king formed a united Maya imperial confederation and adopted the title *Kaloomte’*. The state used Classical Maya as its written language and gradually adopted more lowland cultural features. Mayapán was rebuilt as the capital around 1620.

## Spanish campaigns and the Petén kingdom

Spain ended its alliance with the Maya and conquered the Guatemalan highlands during the 1710s and 1720s. The northern lowlands survived longer through an alliance with Britain, but British support declined after the defeat at Cartagena in 1741. Spain conquered the northern lowlands later in the decade.

The court withdrew to Nojpetén, after which the surviving state was known as the Petén Maya Kingdom. Britain continued to support it as a defense for British Belize, discouraging further large Spanish expeditions.

## Independence and restoration

In 1822, colonial officials, landowners, and military leaders in Guatemala City and Tiho accepted annexation by the Mexica Empire. Maya nationalists rebelled. In 1831, Maya forces expelled the Mexica army and invited the Petén Maya king K’inich Bahlam to rule a restored kingdom from Tiho.

The new kingdom demanded British withdrawal from Belize, beginning a territorial dispute that lasted into the twentieth century.

## Monarchy and reform

The aristocracy initially dominated the restored kingdom. A ruler provisionally identified as King A destroyed the powerful House Alpha in 1847, suppressed a republican uprising in 1855, and greatly reduced aristocratic privileges in 1865. His successor, provisionally identified as King B, promoted modernization and coffee exports after 1871.

K’an Chitam came to the throne in 1888. He initially supported Savi's Pan-Americanism and promoted Classical Maya revival and linguistic purification. As Savi's movement became increasingly Mexica-centered, K’an Chitam moved toward the United States. The Maya entered the First World War against Mexica in 1917.

The [6 B’en Revolution](../history/6-ben-revolution.md) of 1922 restored parliament, restricted royal power, ended Classical Maya as the official literary language, and abolished aristocratic cranial modification. A military ruler provisionally identified as C established an authoritarian regime in 1931. Mass protests removed him in 1944 and reduced the monarchy to a symbolic institution.

## Language and writing

Standard Maya is based on the Yucatec language of Tiho. Classical Maya roots were used extensively to create technical vocabulary. A late nineteenth-century purification movement replaced most Spanish-derived learned vocabulary. The modern national script combines a standardized missionary orthography with elements of the older court tradition.

## Overview

Mexica is a multiethnic republic whose Indigenous population exceeds ninety percent. Even the Nahua, the largest group, do not form an absolute majority. The state considers itself the successor of the Mexica Empire, and many citizens view their country as the principal defender of the Americas against European conquest.

The country includes southern Texas, southern New Mexico, southern Arizona, and southern California. It does not include Yucatán, Quintana Roo, or other territories belonging to the [Kingdom of the Maya](maya.md).

## The expanded Mexica Empire

During the late fifteenth century, the Mexica made extensive use of wheat for military supply and horses for mobility. Under Ahuitzotl, they conquered Tarascan territory and much of the northern Maya lowlands. Under Moctezuma II, they brought most of Aridoamerica into a tributary system and reached their greatest territorial extent.

## Spanish conquest

The first Spanish invasion began in 1519. Cortés advanced toward Tenochtitlan with Totonac and Tlaxcalan allies but was defeated, although Spain retained Veracruz. Cuitláhuac responded by reducing tribute and recognizing greater autonomy for subject peoples, winning back the Totonacs and Tlaxcalans and eventually recovering Veracruz.

Further wars continued throughout the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. Tenochtitlan fell temporarily in 1600, but a government in exile recovered it with Dutch assistance in the 1620s. Spain entered the capital permanently in 1697, ending the empire. For a detailed account, see [Spanish conquest of the Mexica Empire](../history/mexica-conquest.md).

## Independence and the empires

In 1821, Iturbide and the Indigenous noble army cooperated to expel forces loyal to Spain's constitutional government. They soon fought over the political order of the new country. Cacamatzin defeated Iturbide and proclaimed himself tlatoani in 1822, establishing the First Empire. He was deposed in 1826 and replaced by Chimalpilli of the old imperial house, beginning the Second Empire.

The sale of the lower Rio Grande in 1854 united republicans, provincial military leaders, and anti-government nobles. The [Mexica Republican Revolution](../history/mexica-republican-revolution.md) succeeded in 1855, abolished the nobility, and created the First Republic.

## Political traditions

The imperial faction supported monarchy under the old Mexica imperial house and was closely connected to the noble army. The republican movement drew much of its support from poorer Nahua communities and from Zapotec and Mixtec political networks. These alignments shaped the independence conflict, the fall of the Second Empire, and political competition during the First Republic.

## War, Pan-Americanism, and the Third Empire

Mexica lost the [American–Mexica War](../history/american-mexica-war.md) of 1866–1871 after French support collapsed. It ceded Texas and California north of the Tehachapi Mountains.

The Mixtec politician Porforio Savi became president in 1882. He was made president for life in 1892 and proclaimed himself tlatoani in 1895, establishing the Third Empire. Savi promoted modernization and Indigenous Pan-Americanism while suppressing political opposition.

Mexica entered the First World War on the side of the Central Powers in 1916. Defeat in 1918 ended the Third Empire and established the Second Republic. Reparations and military restrictions destabilized the country during the 1920s and early 1930s.

## The Huēyi Teōcalli Incident

In December 1941, one week after the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, the Mexica Nationalist Party attacked the national legislature and the American embassy. President Zempoaltecatl escaped, but the interior minister was killed after being thrown from the Huēyi Teōcalli. The uprising was defeated through cooperation between Mexica and American forces. See [Huēyi Teōcalli Incident](../history/hueyi-teocalli-incident.md).

## Foreign relations

### The Maya

Mexica generally regards the Maya positively as heirs to one of the oldest civilizations of the Americas. This is complicated by a long history of rivalry and memories of the First World War.

### The United States

Public attitudes toward the United States are divided. Some regard it as a historically European-dominated power, while others value the improved position of Indigenous peoples within the federation. Close economic ties make continued cooperation unavoidable.

# United States of America

[Main page](../index.md) > [Countries](index.md) > United States of America

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Government | Federal republic |
| Capital | Washington, D.C. |
| Population | Approximately 290 million |
| Ethnic composition | Indigenous American 60%; European-descended 30%; others |
| Official language | English |
| Other official languages | Creek, Illinois, and many others at state level |
| Writing systems | Latin alphabet, Mississippi scripts, Haudenosaunee script, and others |
| Religion | Predominantly Christian |

## Overview

The United States is a federation of fifty states, but its internal structure differs substantially from that of the real-world United States. It contains one Confederated State, eleven Indigenous Crowned States, and thirty-eight ordinary states. Numerous Indigenous national counties also exist within the ordinary states.

European-descended communities are concentrated along the Atlantic coast and in mining and industrial cities. Many other cities and rural regions have overwhelmingly Indigenous populations.

## Territory

The United States does not include Maine, Michigan, Wisconsin, northern Minnesota, North Dakota, northern Montana, northern Washington, or the Alaska Panhandle, all of which belong to [Vinland](vinland.md). It also lacks southern Texas, southern New Mexico, southern Arizona, and southern California, which belong to [Mexica](mexica.md). Hawaii is an independent country.

## Independence and early expansion

The United States won independence from Britain between 1776 and 1783, with the southern Haudenosaunee federation joining the new republic. Between 1787 and 1796, it allied with the Cherokee, Chickasaw, and Choctaw kingdoms against Coosa and subsequently made them protectorates.

After war with Coosa, the United States acquired the Mobile Bay region in 1800 and incorporated Coosa as a constituent kingdom in 1816. Britain transferred its suzerainty over Kaskaskia to the United States, but Kaskaskia rejected the sale and fought three resistance wars between 1818 and 1829. Natchez, Pacaha, and the Lakota Empire were incorporated during the following decades.

The Western Territories Act of 1841 established a new approach to annexed lands. Existing kingdoms, ethnic autonomies, and strategic river, mining, and transportation districts were initially governed separately rather than being converted directly into ordinary states.

## Western wars

The United States purchased the lower Rio Grande from Mexica in 1854. Victory in the [Oregon War](../history/oregon-war.md) of 1861 brought the Chinook kingdoms and other territories south of the 48th parallel under American control.

During the [American–Mexica War](../history/american-mexica-war.md) of 1866–1871, the United States gained central and northern California as well as suzerainty over the Pueblo, Diné, and Comanche kingdoms. British intervention in the peace negotiations prevented the annexation of all California.

In the 1890s, the federal government dismantled the sovereignty and multiethnic territories of the former kingdoms. Their ethnic heartlands became Crowned States with purely symbolic monarchs, while other territories became ordinary states.

## Twentieth century

Mexica declared war on the United States in 1916. Several southwestern kingdoms rebelled, but most Indigenous peoples in the eastern United States supported the federal government. The United States eventually counterattacked, occupied Tenochtitlan, and also entered the European theater.

The loyalty of most Indigenous citizens strengthened movements for political equality after the war. Restrictions on voting and political participation were gradually removed. In 1976, Gale (Galagina) Saunooke became the first Indigenous president of the United States.

Britain granted the United States a long-term naval lease at Pearl Harbor in 1940. The Japanese attack on the American base in British Hawaii brought the United States into the Second World War.

# Kingdom of Vinland

[Main page](../index.md) > [Countries](index.md) > Kingdom of Vinland

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Government | Constitutional monarchy |
| Capital | Mishilimackinac (Mackinac) |
| Population | Approximately 59 million |
| Ethnic composition | Vinlanders 70%; other Indigenous Americans 20%; others |
| Official language | Vinlandic |
| Writing system | Modern North American runes |
| Religion | Church of Vinland 60%; other Christian traditions 30% |

## Overview

Vinland developed from permanent Norse settlements founded in Newfoundland around the year 1000. Although its founding population came from Europe, prolonged isolation and close interaction with the Indigenous peoples of North America transformed its language, culture, and political institutions. Vinlanders therefore regard themselves as distinct from the European colonists who arrived after the fifteenth century.

## Territory

Compared with real-world Canada, Vinland includes Maine, Michigan, Wisconsin, northern Minnesota, North Dakota, northern Montana, northern Washington, and the Alaska Panhandle. It also includes the autonomous [Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom](../polities/northwest-coast.md).

## Norse settlement and expansion

Norse settlers reached Newfoundland in the early eleventh century with grain, livestock, ironworking specialists, and Eurasian pathogens. As Greenland and transatlantic connections declined, the settlements became increasingly isolated from Europe.

During the eleventh and twelfth centuries, they expanded into southern Labrador and the St. Lawrence basin, establishing trading posts and dependent chiefdoms. Around 1120 they founded a city at Mackinac and established the Kingdom of Vinland. From the mid-twelfth century, Vinlandic groups moved down the Mississippi and founded several states along the river.

## Conflict with the Haudenosaunee and Europe

In the fifteenth century, peoples resisting Vinlandic rule formed the Haudenosaunee Confederacy. The two powers fought repeatedly for control of the Great Lakes.

France established coastal forts in the sixteenth century and later forced Vinland to recognize French navigation rights on the Mitlastchon, or St. Lawrence River. Vinland became a French protectorate in the early eighteenth century and passed to Britain after the French and Indian War.

## Independence and western expansion

An attempt to gain independence during the War of 1812 failed. A new mass independence movement began in 1837, and the Kingdom of Vinland became independent in 1838.

During the nineteenth century, Vinland expanded westward north of the 46th parallel. It entered the [Oregon War](../history/oregon-war.md) in 1861 in support of the Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom, but defeat forced it to surrender Oregon territories to the United States.

Relations deteriorated again during the Alaska controversy of the 1880s. Vinland strongly urged the purchase of Russian Alaska for reasons of national defense, but Russia sold the territory to the United States, partly because it distrusted British-aligned Vinland. American–Vinlandic relations later improved.

# North American Kingdoms and Autonomous Polities

[Main page](../index.md) > [Kingdoms and autonomous polities](index.md) > North American polities

This article summarizes the Indigenous states that shaped North American history and, in many cases, survive as autonomous or Crowned States within the United States and Vinland.

## Modern constitutional position

The United States contains one Confederated State and eleven Indigenous Crowned States. A Crowned State retains a symbolic monarch but otherwise functions within the American federal system. Vinland contains autonomous governments with their own historical institutions.

Known capitals include:

| Polity | Modern status | Capital |
|---|---|---|
| Haudenosaunee | Confederated State in the United States, with a second government in Vinland | TBD |
| Kaskaskia | Crowned State | Grand Kaskaskia (La Vantum), opposite Starved Rock |
| Dinay (Diné) | Crowned State | Naatʼáaniinééz (Shiprock) |
| Pequila (Pueblo) | Crowned State | Kuaua |
| Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom | Autonomous kingdom within Vinland | Ḵ’emḵ’emel̓áy̓ |

## Haudenosaunee Confederacy

In the mid-fourteenth century, a Vinlandic exile provisionally identified as A conquered communities in the Haudenosaunee region and founded the Mitlastchon Kingdom. In the mid-fifteenth century, the Haudenosaunee peoples expelled the king and established a confederated republic.

The confederacy reached its greatest power during the sixteenth century and at times threatened Mackinac, the Vinlandic capital. It declined during the seventeenth century as Mississippi states expanded.

During the American Revolution, the confederacy divided into pro-British and pro-American factions. The pro-American government reunified most of the territory and joined the United States by treaty in the late 1780s, on the condition that its territory and Grand Council be preserved. It later became the only Confederated State in the United States.

The pro-British council and its supporters withdrew north and established a second government in British Vinland. After Vinlandic independence in 1838, it became an autonomous constituent government of Vinland.

For generations the two governments regarded one another as illegitimate. American–Vinlandic reconciliation in the early twentieth century allowed a joint assembly to reunify the traditional Grand Council and clan confederacy without changing borders. The modern Haudenosaunee therefore has one Grand Council but two autonomous governments in two countries.

## Kaskaskia Kingdom

Kaskaskia was an Illinois-speaking Mississippi state descended from a Vinlandic-derived kingdom founded in the mid-twelfth century. It expanded in the late sixteenth century and took the southern shore of Lake Michigan from the Haudenosaunee in the early seventeenth century. Its power peaked in the mid-seventeenth century.

Kaskaskia became a French protectorate in the early eighteenth century. It briefly regained independence after the French and Indian War but was soon annexed by Britain. Britain later transferred its suzerainty to the United States, which Kaskaskia rejected in three resistance wars between 1818 and 1829.

The kingdom ultimately entered the United States while retaining its monarchy. Chicago separated as an ordinary state in the mid-nineteenth century. At the end of the century, non-Illinois territories were removed and the remainder became the Kaskaskia Crowned State.

## Pacaha Kingdom

Pacaha was a Quapaw state of the Mississippi cultural region. It expanded during the early seventeenth century and conquered Natchez. French intervention restored Natchez independence later in the century, and Pacaha became a French protectorate around 1700.

After the French and Indian War, Pacaha remained independent and neutral for several decades. The United States annexed it in the early nineteenth century. At the end of the century, non-Quapaw territories were separated and the ethnic heartland became the Pacaha Crowned State.

## Natchez Kingdom

Natchez was one of the leading Mississippi states during the sixteenth century. Pacaha conquered it in the early seventeenth century, but French intervention restored its independence and made it a French protectorate.

After the French and Indian War it became independent and then entered the Spanish sphere because of its proximity to Spanish territory. Mexica independence briefly restored full independence, but the United States soon annexed the kingdom. The Mississippi delta later passed to Mobile State, and the remaining territory became the Natchez Crowned State.

## Coosa Kingdom

Coosa was a Creek state that reached its greatest power in the early seventeenth century, ruling from Mobile Bay to northern Florida. It declined later in the century but remained independent while neighboring kingdoms accepted French protection.

In the late eighteenth century, the Cherokee, Chickasaw, and Choctaw kingdoms gained independence from Coosa with American support. The United States incorporated Coosa in 1816. Its Creek heartland later became the Coosa Crowned State.

## Cherokee Kingdom

The Cherokee Kingdom became independent from Coosa with American support in the late eighteenth century and soon entered American protection. It adopted a Latin-alphabet orthography and a constitution because Cherokee had not been a major administrative language of the Mississippi states and therefore lacked an established Mississippi-script tradition.

Gold discoveries in Georgia during the 1840s led the federal government to place northern and eastern Cherokee mining districts under direct control. Cherokee resistance was suppressed and major mines passed to white landowners. Unlike in real history, the Indigenous population was too large for a general forced removal.

The kingdom later became a Crowned State. Cherokee workers and landholders in the cotton region achieved comparatively high levels of wealth, and Cherokee citizens became prominent in federal politics. Gale Saunooke became the first Indigenous American president in 1976.

Modern Cherokee living standards are among the highest of any Indigenous people in the United States. Critics sometimes use the ironic expression “honorary white” to describe the resulting distance between Cherokee elites and poorer western Indigenous communities.

## Cofitachequi Kingdom

Cofitachequi was a Catawba state of the Mississippi cultural region. It reached its greatest power in the late seventeenth century, was conquered by Britain in the early eighteenth century, and passed to the United States after American independence.

## Lakota Empires

Between the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, pressure from Vinlandic-derived states and the spread of mounted bison hunting encouraged part of the Dakota population to move onto the Great Plains. These communities developed into the Lakota.

The Lakota united in the early sixteenth century to resist Vinland and established the First Lakota Empire. It dominated much of the Plains and raided the Pueblo and Mississippi regions. An assembly of constituent peoples selected the emperor. The empire divided into northern and southern states at the end of the sixteenth century.

A Second Lakota Empire reunited the peoples in the early eighteenth century. This era also saw the adoption of a Lakota writing system derived from Vinlandic runes. The United States incorporated the empire in the early nineteenth century, and the Lakota heartland became a Crowned State at the end of the century.

## Pueblo Kingdom (Pequila)

The Pueblo Kingdom passed through three dynastic eras.

### First dynasty

In the mid-fifteenth century, a loose Towa-centered confederation formed at Pecos. Athabaskan-speaking Navajo warriors began to serve as mercenaries.

### Second dynasty

In the mid-sixteenth century, a Navajo general who defended the Pueblo region from the Lakota founded a new dynasty. The kingdom reached its greatest power near the end of the century and took tributary territories in Aridoamerica from Mexica. Through intermarriage, the ruling house became culturally Pueblo. The court moved to Kuaua in the mid-seventeenth century and used the incomplete Pueblo Script.

### Third dynasty

A Keres dynasty took power at Kuaua in the late seventeenth century. War with Navajo and Comanche states confined the kingdom largely to the Pueblo region. Mississippi influence contributed to the creation of the Pueblo Syllabary.

Spain incorporated the kingdom into New Spain in the mid-eighteenth century. Pueblo independence movements failed during Mexica independence in 1821 and the Republican Revolution in 1855, but the region retained autonomy and a restored monarch.

The [American–Mexica War](../history/american-mexica-war.md) transferred the region to the United States in 1871. American attempts to restrict local authority generated strong resistance. A Pan-Americanist independence movement emerged in the 1890s, and the pro-Mexica king was removed and exiled.

In 1916, the Pueblo government entered the First World War after Mexica promised postwar independence. American victory returned the region to federal control.

# 4 Kimi Incident

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > 4 Kimi Incident

The 4 Kimi Incident was an assassination attempt against the Maya ruler K’an Chitam on 16 July 1910.

A young Maya Pan-Americanist, inspired by Porforio Savi and advocating political unity with Mexica, attacked the king with a concealed obsidian knife. K’an Chitam was seriously wounded but survived.

The incident was named for its date in the Tzolk’in calendar. Its use of an obsidian weapon gave the attack powerful cultural symbolism at a time when obsidian had long disappeared from ordinary warfare.

The assassination attempt deepened the political split between K’an Chitam and Savi and contributed to the Maya decision to oppose Mexica during the First World War.

# 6 B’en Revolution

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > 6 B’en Revolution

The 6 B’en Revolution of 28 October 1922 transformed the Kingdom of the Maya from an authoritarian monarchy into a constitutional state.

## Background

K’an Chitam's government was criticized after the First World War as an outdated absolute monarchy. Although the Maya had joined the victorious Allies, it did not receive the claimed Maya-inhabited territory of central Tabasco. The apparent diplomatic failure weakened the prestige of the king.

## Revolution

On 28 October 1922, citizens gathered in Tiho and demanded a new constitution and the restoration of representative government. The revolution took its name from the day's position in the Tzolk’in calendar.

K’an Chitam appealed to the United States for support. Washington no longer regarded his monarchy as an essential postwar ally and refused to intervene. The king accepted most of the protesters' demands, abdicated, and transferred the throne to his son.

## Reforms

The revolution:

- restored the long-marginalized parliament;
- enacted a new constitution limiting royal authority;
- ended Classical Maya as the official literary language;
- abolished artificial cranial modification among the royal and aristocratic classes.

The war had greatly increased the prestige of the armed forces. Although the revolution limited the monarchy, it was followed by an era of military governments and coups.

# American–Mexica War

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > American–Mexica War

The American–Mexica War of 1866–1871 was a prolonged conflict between the United States and the First Mexica Republic. French support initially prevented an American breakthrough, but the collapse of that support during the Franco-Prussian War led to a Mexica defeat.

## Background

The discovery of gold in California in 1849 brought immigrants from Mexica, the United States, Vinland, and elsewhere. American interest in California intensified. In 1862, Napoleon III offered a mutual guarantee intended to protect Mexica and French interests in California.

## War

War began in 1866. French assistance and strong Mexica resistance prevented the United States from achieving a decisive victory, and the front stabilized. The Franco-Prussian War forced France to withdraw in 1871, leaving Mexica isolated.

## Peace settlement

The United States won the war, but British intervention prevented it from annexing all California. Mexica ceded Texas and California north of the Tehachapi Mountains. The United States also acquired suzerainty over the Pueblo, Diné, and Comanche kingdoms.

American rule in the southwest proved more centralized and intrusive than earlier Mexica rule. Initial support for liberation among some Pueblo communities gave way to resistance. The war's aftermath destabilized Mexica politics and helped create the conditions for Savi's later rise.

# Huēyi Teōcalli Incident

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Huēyi Teōcalli Incident

The Huēyi Teōcalli Incident was an attempted seizure of power by the Mexica Nationalist Party in December 1941, one week after the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor.

## Background

Reparations and military restrictions imposed after the First World War destabilized Mexica during the 1920s. The Great Depression worsened the crisis, allowing the Nazi-influenced Mexica Nationalist Party to grow and organize attacks on Americans.

American financial concessions and investment improved the economy during the late 1930s and slowed the party's growth. When the Second World War began in Europe in 1939, the government banned the organization, but it continued underground.

## Attack

The Nationalist Party used its private militia to enter Tenochtitlan and attack the national legislature and the American embassy. The small Mexica military, still constrained by the postwar settlement, could not respond effectively at first.

President Zempoaltecatl escaped. The interior minister was captured and killed by being thrown from the Huēyi Teōcalli, also known as the Templo Mayor.

## Suppression and legacy

Mexica troops defeated the uprising with American military assistance. The incident became an important moment in the later reconciliation between the two countries. After the Second World War, Mexica was allowed to expand its armed forces.

# Spanish Conquest of the Mexica Empire

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Spanish conquest of the Mexica Empire

The Spanish conquest of the Mexica Empire was a series of wars between 1519 and 1697. Unlike the conquest of the historical Aztec Empire, Spain could not rely on overwhelming epidemic collapse or a stable coalition of dissatisfied tributary states. The result was nearly two centuries of intermittent warfare.

## First invasion

In 1519, Hernán Cortés advanced toward Tenochtitlan with Totonac and Tlaxcalan allies. He failed to take the capital but retained Veracruz. After Moctezuma II's death, Cuitláhuac reduced tribute, recognized greater local autonomy, and rebuilt the imperial alliance. The Totonacs and Tlaxcalans returned to Mexica allegiance, and Veracruz was recovered during the 1520s.

Spain attempted to retake Veracruz with regular troops in the 1530s but withdrew after failing to obtain sufficient local allies.

## War on the isthmus

In the 1540s, Spain used a lowland Maya revolt to renew the war. K’iche’ intervention helped the lowlands gain independence from Mexica, while Spain secured Coatzacoalcos as a base on the isthmus.

From the 1550s through the 1570s, Spanish forces advanced with allies from the isthmus and the united Maya realm. Mexica cavalry prevented them from taking Tenochtitlan. After the fall of Cusco freed additional Spanish forces, a larger offensive approached the capital during the 1580s and 1590s.

## First fall of Tenochtitlan

Tenochtitlan fell in 1600. Emperor Tezozomoc, the imperial family, and the priesthood withdrew toward Metztitlan and maintained a government in exile.

Between 1621 and 1629, the exiled government allied with the Dutch and recovered the capital. Itzcoatl II entered Tenochtitlan in 1623. Attempts to expel Spain completely failed, leaving three broad zones of power:

- Mexica controlled the Basin of Mexico, the central plateau, and Michoacán.
- Spain controlled Veracruz, eastern Puebla, Oaxaca, and Tehuantepec.
- The Dutch controlled fortified positions around Tuxpan and Pánuco.

## End of Dutch support

In 1648, the Dutch declined to renew their military alliance with Mexica and excluded it from the protection of the Peace of Münster. Spain recognized Dutch possession of the Gulf fortresses, while the Dutch ended military support for Tenochtitlan.

As Dutch neutrality hardened during the late seventeenth century, Spain gradually advanced. Tlaxcala submitted in 1680, although the fortified lake zone delayed further conquest. Tarascan territory fell in 1690.

## Final conquest

Spanish forces entered Tenochtitlan in 1697, ending the Mexica Empire. During the War of the Spanish Succession, Spain captured the remaining Dutch fortresses. In the 1714 settlement, the Dutch abandoned their claims and recognized Spanish possession.

The long conquest preserved many Indigenous nobles, military institutions, and local communities. These later became central to the [Mexica independence revolution](mexica-independence.md).

# Mexica Independence Revolution

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Mexica independence revolution

The Mexica independence revolution began during the Spanish constitutional crisis of the early 1820s. It first united royalist officer Agustín de Iturbide with the Mexica noble army, but their incompatible plans for the postcolonial state soon produced a civil war.

## Background

The Mexica noble army was composed of Indigenous nobles, many descended from royal and aristocratic families that had changed sides during the seventeenth-century conquest. It defended frontier regions in place of regular Spanish forces and accumulated increasing political and military authority.

In 1810, the Otomi leader Ndähi (Endai) rebelled in Guanajuato. Colonial troops and the Mexica noble army suppressed the uprising.

## Alliance against the constitutional government

When Spain underwent a constitutional revolution, Iturbide and the noble army shared an interest in removing forces loyal to the new constitutional order. The alliance succeeded in expelling them in 1821.

The victors then disagreed over sovereignty. Iturbide regarded the Mexica emperor as subordinate to the king of Spain and hoped to invite a Bourbon prince. The noble army wanted the Mexica tlatoani to hold the highest authority in the new country.

## Civil war and the First Empire

Cacamatzin, a talented officer from the middle nobility, defeated Iturbide and took control of the noble army. Many aristocrats wanted Chimalpilli of the old imperial house to become emperor, while Cacamatzin's faction supported its own commander.

In 1822, Cacamatzin proclaimed himself tlatoani and expelled rival factions, establishing the First Mexica Empire. His lack of dynastic ancestry undermined his position. The imperial faction deposed him in 1826 and placed Chimalpilli on the throne, beginning the Second Empire.

# Mexica Republican Revolution

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Mexica Republican Revolution

The Mexica Republican Revolution of 1855 overthrew the Second Empire, abolished the nobility, and established the First Republic.

## Background

The Second Empire preserved the aristocratic order inherited from the colonial period. Republican opposition was strongest among poorer Nahua communities, as well as Zapotec and Mixtec political networks. Cecuiztli Tocal emerged as one of the principal organizers of this movement during the 1840s.

The monarchy also suffered from religious controversy. During the 1830s, a priest related to the imperial family was discovered to have sacrificed a child to Tlaloc. The incident strengthened criticism of the political privileges retained by nobles and priests.

## The lower Rio Grande sale

The immediate catalyst was the imperial government's sale of the lower Rio Grande to the United States in 1854. The government argued that it lacked the resources to defend both California and the unstable northeastern frontier. Opponents condemned the decision as the sale of national territory.

Republicans, provincial officers, and anti-government nobles formed a common revolutionary coalition. In 1855, they deposed Tlatoani Tezozomoc, abolished the aristocracy, and proclaimed the First Republic. Cecuiztli Tocal became its first president.

## Legacy

The revolution accelerated the decline of elite customs such as artificial cranial modification in Mexica. It did not, however, resolve ethnic and regional conflicts. Political instability after the American–Mexica War later helped the rise of Porforio Savi and the Third Empire.

# Oregon War

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Oregon War

The Oregon War of 1861 was fought between the United States and Vinland, with the Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom allied to Vinland. It determined the modern border between American and Vinlandic influence on the Pacific coast.

## Background

The Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom united Chinook and other kingdoms from the Fraser River to the Tsimshian region during the 1770s. Haida and Tlingit members joined over the following decades. Vinland's nineteenth-century westward expansion brought it into close relations with the confederation.

American and Vinlandic claims overlapped in Oregon. The confederation became the immediate focus of the dispute and allied with Vinland.

## War and settlement

The United States defeated the allied forces in 1861. Chinook and other constituent kingdoms south of the 48th parallel passed to the United States. The remaining Northwest Coast Confederated Kingdom became a Vinlandic protectorate.

The war limited Vinlandic expansion and established the 48th parallel as the principal border in the region. During renewed tension over Alaska in the 1880s, the confederation formally entered the Kingdom of Vinland while retaining its monarchy and internal autonomy.

# Transmission of Technology, Crops, Animals, and Disease

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > Transmission of technology, crops, animals, and disease

## Norse settlement

Norse settlers reached Newfoundland in the early eleventh century. Unlike the real-world settlement at L’Anse aux Meadows, their colony included grain, sheep, horses, and specialists capable of maintaining iron production. Several Eurasian pathogens also arrived with them.

By the twelfth century, Norse communities had reached the Great Lakes and founded the [Kingdom of Vinland](../countries/vinland.md). From the mid-twelfth century, Vinlandic groups moved down the Mississippi and established several states along the main river. These communities and their trade networks became the main early channels through which Eurasian biological and technological elements spread across the Americas.

## Disease

The first major epidemics of Eurasian origin affected North America and Mesoamerica during the twelfth century and South America during the thirteenth. Further outbreaks followed. By the time of Columbus, much of the population had experienced repeated exposure, creating a degree of collective resistance.

This did not prevent severe epidemics, but it meant that European colonization did not produce the sudden, continent-wide demographic collapse of real history. Indigenous states retained enough population and institutional continuity to resist, negotiate, and survive.

## Horses

Some Norse horses escaped or were released and formed feral populations. Others spread through trade, raiding, and deliberate breeding.

- **Mid-eleventh century:** Great Lakes.
- **Mid-twelfth century:** Mississippi basin and Great Plains.
- **Mid-thirteenth century:** Mesoamerica.
- **Late fourteenth century:** Andes.

Mounted hunting and warfare transformed the Plains and helped create the conditions for the Lakota empires. In Mesoamerica, cavalry and pack transport contributed to Mexica expansion. In the Andes, horses arrived later and their military impact remained more limited.

## Wheat

Wheat moved primarily through Vinlandic-derived states and reached the Mississippi civilization in the mid-twelfth century. It was especially useful in cooler and drier regions and could be grown at different times from many Indigenous crops.

Wheat reached Mesoamerica by the late fourteenth century. Its durability and ease of transport made it valuable for military supply and supported the expansion of the Mexica Empire.

## Ironworking

Ironworking reached the Mississippi basin through Vinlandic-derived states in the mid-twelfth century. Its spread was slower than that of horses or finished iron objects because it depended on ore, fuel, furnaces, and trained specialists.

### Iron in Mesoamerica

- **Mid-twelfth century:** Vinlandic expeditions raid parts of the Mississippi basin.
- **c. 1200:** Vinlandic raiders use iron weapons in the Huastec region, the first known appearance of iron objects in Mesoamerica.
- **Early thirteenth century:** A Vinlandic-derived state near the Mississippi delta expands trade in iron objects with Mesoamerica.
- **By c. 1300:** Most major Mesoamerican states use imported Mississippi iron, although obsidian remains the principal military material.
- **Early fourteenth century:** Tarascan smiths begin the first local iron production in Mesoamerica.
- **Late fourteenth century:** Other Mesoamerican states begin local production.
- **By the Spanish arrival:** Iron is common in warfare and agriculture, although obsidian retains important ritual uses.

## Historical consequences

These processes placed the divergence of TMTW centuries before Columbus. North American states became larger and more durable, Mesoamerican states resisted Spanish conquest into the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries, and the survival of Indigenous populations reshaped colonial and postcolonial South America.

# First World War in the Americas

[Main page](../index.md) > [Historical events](index.md) > First World War in the Americas

The First World War spread directly into North America after the Third Mexica Empire entered the conflict on the side of the Central Powers in 1916. Mexica declared war on the United States under the banner of liberating the Indigenous peoples of the Americas.

## Mexica strategy

Porforio Savi accepted the proposal associated with German foreign minister Arthur Zimmermann and presented the war as a continental Indigenous struggle. Mexica forces quickly occupied Pueblo territory and advanced into Texas and the American Southwest.

Several southwestern kingdoms rebelled after receiving promises of postwar independence. The Pueblo government joined the Central Powers after Mexica guaranteed its restoration.

## American response

Mexica planners expected Indigenous communities throughout the United States to revolt. Instead, most Indigenous peoples in the eastern United States supported the federal government. This prevented a wider collapse and allowed the United States to stabilize the front, counterattack, and eventually occupy Tenochtitlan.

The United States also entered the European theater.

## Maya entry

K’an Chitam had once supported Savi's Pan-Americanism but rejected its increasingly Mexica-centered form. The Kingdom of the Maya declared war on Mexica in 1917 and joined the Allied side.

## Consequences

Mexica was defeated in 1918. The Third Empire was abolished, the Second Republic was created, and Savi was confined to an estate outside Tenochtitlan. Mexica retained its territory but faced long-term reparations and military restrictions.

Within the United States, the loyalty of most Indigenous citizens increased their political influence and accelerated movements against voting restrictions. In the Maya kingdom, military victory strengthened the armed forces, while disappointment with the postwar settlement contributed to the [6 B’en Revolution](6-ben-revolution.md).

# Inti Religion

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Inti religion

The Inti religion is an Andean faith that developed from historical Inkarri beliefs. It recognizes Inti as the highest deity and teaches that the Sapa Inca will one day return to save the people.

## Formation

The religion emerged in an organized form around 1700, after the destruction of the last New Inca state. Its scriptures were written in Quechua using the Latin alphabet, while quipu played an important role in ritual.

The movement was heavily influenced by Christianity. Its teaching about a returning Sapa Inca and its accommodation with localized Catholic practice made it difficult for colonial authorities to define and suppress. In many communities, the boundary between Inti practice and local Catholicism remained unclear.

## Political role

Túpac Amaru III's uprising of 1780–1783 attempted a radical restoration of Inca institutions, including quipu administration. Its failure later encouraged more moderate Inti political leaders.

José Mariano Chuquisengo established a moderate Inti republic in Peru after independence. He refused to claim the title Sapa Inca and balanced religious legitimacy against modern government. In 1826, he established the Sacred Quipu script as a compromise between doctrine and administrative practicality.

The religion became a major source of common identity for Peru, Bolivia, and Ecuador during the formation of the [Andean Federation](../countries/andean-federation.md).

## Reform and secularization

The Inti Church and the federal government were initially strongly Quechua-centered. Aymara and other revolts during the 1850s challenged this structure. The federation later reorganized into ethnic and regional states.

During the 1890s, the state restricted the political authority of the Inti Church and introduced freedom of religion and secular education.

# Languages of the Americas

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Languages

The survival of large Indigenous populations and states allowed many American languages to become modern national and administrative languages. They possess standardized forms, technical vocabulary, mass media, and established systems of education.

## Nahuatl

Nahuatl is an official language of [Mexica](../countries/mexica.md). Its dialects have approximately 90 million first-language speakers and 70 million second-language speakers. It is also widely spoken by Mexica migrant communities, especially in the southwestern United States.

The standard language is based on the Tenochtitlan dialect and is used in newspapers and broadcasting. The Latin alphabet is the normal writing system, while Mexica writing sometimes appears in ceremonies and public monuments.

Its grammar does not differ radically from real-world Nahuatl, but it has a large modern administrative and technical vocabulary. Spanish loans remain common in learned registers.

## Standard Maya

Standard Maya is the official language of the [Kingdom of the Maya](../countries/maya.md). It has approximately 8 million first-language speakers, but almost the entire population of roughly 40 million can use it as a first or second language.

The standard is based on Yucatec Maya, especially the dialect of Tiho. It is normally written in the modern national Maya script, which is used in books and newspapers.

Its grammar remains broadly comparable to real-world Maya languages. Technical and administrative terms were created largely from Classical Maya roots, especially Ch’orti’-related material. This produced many pairs of everyday and learned words. A late nineteenth-century language purification movement replaced most Spanish loanwords in learned vocabulary.

## Vinlandic

Vinlandic is the official language of the [Kingdom of Vinland](../countries/vinland.md). It has approximately 30 million first-language speakers and 7 million second-language speakers.

The language descends from Old Norse as spoken by settlers who arrived around the year 1000. A millennium of contact with Algonquian and other North American languages produced features uncommon in European languages, including polysynthetic verbal structures and possessive prefixes.

Vinlandic is normally written in North American runes, although the Latin alphabet is also used. Much modern vocabulary has been borrowed from French and English.

## English

English is the official language of the [United States](../countries/united-states.md). It contains a large number of regional varieties shaped by Creek, Illinois, and many other Indigenous languages. The strongest varieties can be difficult for speakers of more international forms of English to understand.

## Other major languages

Spanish, Quechua, Mapuche, Guaraní, Nheengatu, and many North American Indigenous languages hold national, state, or regional status. Their detailed profiles remain to be expanded.

# Mesoamerican Customs

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Mesoamerican customs

This article describes practices that survived far longer in TMTW because Mesoamerican states and elite institutions remained intact into the modern period.

## Artificial cranial modification

Artificial cranial modification reshapes an infant's skull with boards or bands while the bone is still soft. In real history, the practice declined after the sixteenth-century conquest. In TMTW it survived into modern times.

Spanish colonial authorities attempted to prohibit it during the eighteenth century but abandoned enforcement in the face of strong local opposition. In Mexica it declined rapidly after the Republican Revolution of 1855. In the Maya kingdom it remained a mark of royal and aristocratic status into the twentieth century.

Maya intellectuals and European observers strongly criticized the practice. The 6 B’en Revolution of 1922 forced K’an Chitam to abolish it among the royal family and nobility. Some remote communities continue the practice in the present day.

## Human sacrifice

Human sacrifice also survived longer than in real history, especially in remote regions. Spanish colonial law officially prohibited it, but authorities sometimes ignored ceremonies performed by powerful nobles.

Both Mexica and the Maya prohibited the practice after independence. During the 1830s, however, a priest related to the Mexica imperial family was discovered to have sacrificed a child to Tlaloc. The scandal became one of the sources of opposition to the Second Empire.

By the twentieth century, human sacrifice was believed to have disappeared. In 1950, a United Nations investigation discovered that it continued in parts of the Petén region. The Maya government launched investigations, arrested priests, and conducted public education campaigns. The practice is no longer believed to occur.

## Obsidian

Iron gradually replaced obsidian in ordinary warfare, but obsidian retained ritual and political symbolism. The assassin in the [4 Kimi Incident](../history/4-kimi-incident.md) deliberately used an obsidian knife, giving the attack a connection to older Mesoamerican traditions.

# Writing Systems of the Americas

[Main page](../index.md) > [Languages and culture](index.md) > Writing systems

TMTW contains several living writing traditions that either survived from pre-Columbian civilizations or developed from contact between Norse and Indigenous systems.

## Maya writing

Unlike in real history, Maya writing remains in everyday use in books, newspapers, education, and public life. Its modern form differs from the historical script.

When the united Maya state formed in the late sixteenth century, it adopted Classical Maya as its written language and standardized regional graphic forms. Variant signs were reduced.

After Spanish colonization in the eighteenth century, missionaries used Maya writing as a tool of evangelization. They selected one sign for each syllabic value and created a standardized missionary orthography. The royal court and aristocracy continued to use a more traditional system.

After Maya independence in the 1830s, the missionary standard and court tradition were combined into the National Maya script for mass education.

## North American runes

The runes brought by Norse settlers had too few letters to distinguish all sounds. Like real-world medieval runes, the North American system developed diacritics to separate different values.

It also developed conventions unlike the Latin alphabet. For example, syllable-final *n* can be written with a diacritic. The modern system is used primarily for Vinlandic.

## Mississippi scripts

The Mississippi scripts developed from runes during the fourteenth century. They diversified greatly during the political height of the Mississippi kingdoms around 1700.

Most remain alphabetic in principle but arrange consonants and vowels vertically inside compact syllable blocks, producing a visual appearance comparable to Maya glyph blocks. Regional versions differ considerably. Some enlarge verbal roots to distinguish them from affixes, while others include a limited number of logograms.

Mississippi-derived traditions influenced the Pueblo Script, Pueblo Syllabary, and the Lakota adaptation of runes.

## Mexica writing

Mexica writing survives mainly in ritual use and on public monuments. Ordinary administration and publishing normally use the Latin alphabet. A fuller description of its modern standardized form remains to be developed.

## Sacred Quipu script

The [Andean Federation](../countries/andean-federation.md) established the Sacred Quipu script in 1826. It was designed as a compromise between the Inti religious expectation that a restored Inca society would use quipu and the practical needs of a modern state. It is used alongside Latin-alphabet Quechua in religious and symbolic contexts.

# Cacamatzin Chimal

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > Cacamatzin Chimal

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Lifespan | 1779–1828 |
| Position | Tlatoani of Mexica, 1822–1826 |
| Known for | Victory in the independence civil war; founder of the First Empire |

Cacamatzin Chimal was born into a family of the middle Mexica nobility. A talented military commander, he defeated Iturbide during the [Mexica independence revolution](../history/mexica-independence.md) and took control of the Mexica noble army.

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > Cecuiztli Tocal

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Lifespan | 1805–1887 |

# José Mariano Chuquisengo

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > José Mariano Chuquisengo

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Lifespan | 1769–1840 |
| Roles | Religious leader and politician in Peru and the Andean Federation |
| Known for | Peruvian independence; moderate Inti republicanism; Sacred Quipu script |

José Mariano Chuquisengo was born into a northern Quechua family of Chachapoya ancestry and moved to Cusco while young.

## Independence

Chuquisengo declared independence in the Peruvian highlands in 1821. His initial attempt to advance on Lima failed, and he requested assistance from Bolívar. With that support, Peruvian independence was secured in 1824.

Although the new state initially had a strong religious character, Chuquisengo rejected the radical restoration attempted by Túpac Amaru III. He sought a balance between Inti doctrine and practical modern government and never claimed the title of Sapa Inca.

## Sacred Quipu script

In 1826, Chuquisengo established the Sacred Quipu script. The system served as a compromise with the doctrine that the restored realm of the Sapa Inca would use quipu, while allowing the state to maintain modern written administration.

## Death

Chuquisengo died in 1840 during the war between the Andean Federation, Chile, and anti-federal forces.

# K’an Chitam

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > K’an Chitam

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Lifespan | 1858–1927 |
| Reign | 1888–1922 |
| Title | Kaloomte’ of the Maya |

K’an Chitam became ruler of the Maya at the age of thirty after the sudden death of his predecessor.

## Pan-Americanism and cultural policy

During the early part of his reign, K’an Chitam supported Savi's Indigenous Pan-Americanism. He promoted a revival of Classical Maya culture and a language purification campaign that replaced Spanish-derived learned vocabulary with terms built from Classical Maya roots.

After Savi proclaimed himself tlatoani, Mexica-centered elements of the movement became more visible. K’an Chitam distanced himself from Savi and moved closer to the United States.

## 4 Kimi Incident and war

In 1910, a young Maya Pan-Americanist inspired by Savi attacked K’an Chitam with an obsidian knife. The king was seriously wounded. The [4 Kimi Incident](../history/4-kimi-incident.md) deepened the conflict between the Maya court and Savi.

K’an Chitam entered the First World War against Mexica in 1917.

## Abdication

Although the Maya emerged on the victorious side, the absolute monarchy appeared increasingly anachronistic. The failure to acquire Maya-inhabited parts of central Tabasco further weakened the king.

The [6 B’en Revolution](../history/6-ben-revolution.md) of 1922 forced K’an Chitam to accept constitutional reforms and abdicate in favor of his son.

# Rulers of Mexica

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > Rulers of Mexica

This list contains the rulers currently established in the setting. Gaps in the succession remain to be developed.

## Late imperial period

### Cuitláhuac

**Reigned:** 1520–1532

Cuitláhuac responded to the first Spanish invasion by reducing tribute and granting greater autonomy to subject states. His reforms held the imperial alliance together and allowed Mexica to recover Veracruz.

### Cuauhtémoc

**Reigned:** 1532–1561

Cuauhtémoc defeated a Spanish attempt to recover Veracruz and became a hero of the First Mexica War.

### Tehuetzquititzin

**Reigned:** 1561–1584

The current setting has not yet established a fuller biography.

### Tezozomoc

**Reigned:** 1584–1612

After the first fall of Tenochtitlan in 1600, Tezozomoc established a government in exile at Metztitlan.

### Itzcoatl II

**Reigned:** 1612–1639

Itzcoatl II recovered Tenochtitlan with Dutch support in 1623. The historical inspiration for the character is Pedro Tesifón de Moctezuma.

## Independent Mexica

### Cacamatzin Chimal

**Lived:** 1779–1828  
**Reigned:** 1822–1826

Cacamatzin won the independence civil war and founded the First Empire. See [Cacamatzin Chimal](cacamatzin-chimal.md).

### Chimalpilli

**Lived:** 1778–1835  
**Reigned:** 1826–1835

Chimalpilli was the twenty-seventh head of the old Mexica imperial house and the first ruler of the Second Empire.

### Unnamed second ruler of the Second Empire

The ruler between Chimalpilli and Tezozomoc has not yet been named.

### Tezozomoc

**Lived:** 1801–1862  
**Reigned:** 1851–1855

Tezozomoc was the third and final ruler of the Second Empire. He was Chimalpilli's younger son and succeeded after the death of his elder brother. His sale of the lower Rio Grande in 1854 helped trigger the Republican Revolution.

Chimalpilli was assassinated during an aristocratic factional struggle when Tezozomoc was thirty-four. Tezozomoc did not inherit immediately because his elder brother first succeeded to the throne; he became tlatoani only after that brother's death.

### Porforio Savi

**Lived:** 1841–1921  
**Reigned as tlatoani:** 1895–1918

Savi founded the Third Empire and led Mexica into the First World War. See [Porforio Savi](porforio-savi.md).

# Porforio Savi

[Main page](../index.md) > [People](index.md) > Porforio Savi

| Category | Information |
|---|---|
| Lifespan | 1841–1921 |
| Offices | President of Mexica; president for life; tlatoani of the Third Empire |
| Political movement | Indigenous Pan-Americanism |

Porforio Savi was a Mixtec military officer, politician, and ruler of Mexica. He was born into a Mixtec family in Oaxaca and served under the Zapotec commander Bidxiña during the American–Mexica War and the instability that followed.

## Rise to power

Savi became a general but concluded that Bidxiña's government could not overcome ethnic and political division. He entered politics under the banner of Indigenous Pan-Americanism and became president in 1882.

His government modernized Mexica while suppressing opponents and adopting an increasingly hostile position toward European-descended powers, especially the United States. Savi amended the constitution to become president for life in 1892 and proclaimed himself tlatoani in 1895, establishing the Third Empire.

## First World War

In 1916, Savi accepted the German proposal associated with the Zimmermann Telegram and entered the war on the side of the Central Powers. He proclaimed the liberation of all Indigenous peoples in the Americas. Mexica initially occupied the Pueblo region and parts of Texas, but most Indigenous peoples in the eastern United States remained loyal to Washington.

American forces eventually defeated Mexica and occupied Tenochtitlan. The Third Empire was abolished in 1918, and Savi was confined to an estate outside the capital.

## Legacy

Savi's statue in Tenochtitlan was removed after the war. Its possible reconstruction remains politically controversial, and right-wing parties periodically call for its return.
